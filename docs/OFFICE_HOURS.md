# Office Hours — GoodStride

**Mode:** Startup, pre-product. **Position:** The broad “all marathon inventory” marketplace is too wide. The paid wedge is post-lottery NYC runners who want a legitimate charity path and need commitment clarity fast.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** Market data is a signal, not demand. Strongest proxy: runners already accept charity minimums and pay tour operators for guaranteed entries. Missing proof: a runner paying a finder fee or a charity paying for placement.
2. **Status quo:** Rejected runners open hundreds of charity pages, spreadsheets, and social posts; minimums, deadlines, and availability are inconsistent and stale. Small charities answer repetitive eligibility questions and struggle to fill remaining places.
3. **Desperate customer:** A time-qualified runner rejected from the NYC lottery, with travel dates held and willingness to raise $3k–$5k, racing against charity deadlines. Buyer-side counterpart: a small charity’s events manager measured on filled places and net funds raised.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** A verified NYC-only board that answers: “Which charity places are still open, what am I committing to, and can I fundraise it by the deadline?” Charge charities only after proving qualified introductions.
5. **Observation plan:** Watch 8 rejected runners find and compare a place without guidance; shadow 5 charity event managers updating inventory. Record source switching, confusion, abandonment, and what “verified” must mean.
6. **Future-fit:** As lottery demand and fragmented charity programs grow, verified availability becomes more valuable. If race organizers centralize real-time inventory, the directory commoditizes; durable value must shift to qualification, fundraising readiness, and conversion workflow.

## Premise challenge

- The customer outcome is not “browse charities”; it is “secure a legitimate entry without accepting a fundraising obligation blindly.”
- Doing nothing costs charities unfilled allocations and runners lost time, but that pain must be measured before building a marketplace.
- Supply freshness is the product. A beautiful directory with stale inventory destroys trust.

## Approaches considered

| Approach | Effort | Risk | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Concierge NYC board | S | Low | Airtable/manual verification; fastest demand test and recommended wedge. |
| Self-serve multi-race marketplace | M | High | Better coverage, but cold-start and stale-inventory risks compound. |
| Organizer inventory API | XL | High | Ideal architecture later; requires institutional partnerships and standardized feeds. |

**Recommendation:** Concierge NYC board. The prototype demonstrates discovery plus commitment planning, while explicitly avoiding claims of live supply.

## Assignment

Within 48 hours of NYC lottery results, contact 20 smaller participating charities and recruit 5 runners for observed searches. Success is 10 verified listings, 3 qualified introductions, and one party willing to pay—not waitlist volume.
